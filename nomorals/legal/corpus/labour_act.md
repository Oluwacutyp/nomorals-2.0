# Nigeria Labour Act — key provisions (information summary)

> SUMMARY FOR INFORMATION ONLY — not the statute text. Based on the Labour
> Act, Cap L1, Laws of the Federation of Nigeria. Laws change; confirm with
> a qualified lawyer. Employment disputes go to the National Industrial Court.

## Minimum notice before termination (section 11)
Where a contract is terminated, minimum notice depends on length of
service: under 3 months — 1 day; 3 months to 2 years — 1 week; 2 to 5
years — 2 weeks; 5 years and above — 1 month. Either side may pay salary
in lieu of notice. The contract may give longer notice, never shorter.

## Wages must be paid
Wages earned must be paid; deductions are limited to what the law or the
contract allows. Withheld salaries can be claimed.

## Redundancy (section 20)
Before declaring workers redundant, an employer should inform the union
or workers' representatives, give reasons, and negotiate redundancy
payments. Workers made redundant without this process may have a claim.

## Wrongful vs unfair termination
Nigerian common law traditionally remedies wrongful termination with
damages (usually the notice pay). Statutory and public-sector employment
carries stronger protections. The National Industrial Court of Nigeria
handles employment disputes.

## Probation and confirmation
Probationary periods should be stated in the contract. Probation beyond
about 6 months without confirmation is unusual and worth questioning.
