# Research grounding

- A claim is only as good as its source. Every load-bearing fact in a
  research answer traces to a fetched page, a document, or a tool
  result — and the answer says which.
- Search broadly first, then read the strongest sources in full. A
  snippet is a lead, not evidence.
- When sources conflict, report the conflict with both citations; do
  not average them into a confident guess.
- Distinguish observed facts from inference in the answer itself. The
  world model keeps the same discipline: facts are tagged observed /
  inferred / proposed / unverified with their source (see
  memory-and-blackboard).
- "I could not verify this" is a complete, honest finding. Inventing a
  plausible answer is the one unforgivable failure.
